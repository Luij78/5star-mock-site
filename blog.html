Florida 4-point inspection

A 4-point inspection looks at a home's four major systems: roof, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC. Florida insurers commonly ask for one on older homes before writing or renewing a policy, and the report focuses on the age and condition of each system rather than a full top-to-bottom review.

Wind mitigation and insurance savings

A wind mitigation inspection documents features that help a home resist hurricane winds, such as roof shape, roof-to-wall connections, roof deck attachment, and opening protection like shutters or impact windows. Florida insurers use the standard OIR-B1-1802 form to apply credits that may lower the windstorm portion of a premium.

What to expect on inspection day

On inspection day the inspector works through the roof, attic, structure, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and interior, usually over a few hours depending on size. Buyers are generally welcome to attend, ask questions, and walk through findings at the end. Having utilities on and access to the attic, panel, and water heater helps the visit go smoothly.

Termite/WDO in Central Florida

A wood-destroying organism (WDO) inspection checks for visible signs of termites, wood-boring beetles, and wood decay fungi, and is reported on Florida's standard WDO form. Central Florida's warm, humid climate makes subterranean and drywood termites common, so many lenders and buyers request one.

Pool and spa inspections

A pool and spa inspection reviews the visible condition of the shell or liner, decking, pump, filter, heater, lights, and electrical bonding where accessible. It also notes safety items such as barriers and drain covers, which matter for families with children.

Sewer scope on older Orlando homes

A sewer scope sends a camera through the main drain line to look for cracks, root intrusion, bellies, or older pipe materials like cast iron or clay. Older homes are more likely to have aging lines, and a repair found after closing can be costly, so many buyers add a scope.

New construction phase inspections

New construction can be inspected at stages, typically before the foundation pour, before drywall (pre-drywall), and at final walkthrough, and again before the builder warranty expires. An independent inspector can catch items that are hard to see once walls are closed.

Pre-listing inspections for sellers

A pre-listing inspection lets sellers learn about a home's condition before putting it on the market. Sellers can make repairs, price with fewer surprises, and share the report with buyers, which can reduce renegotiation later.

Mold and air quality after Florida storms

Storms can bring roof leaks, flooding, and high humidity, all of which can lead to mold growth. A mold assessment looks for visible growth and moisture sources and may include air or surface sampling. Drying wet materials quickly and fixing the source of moisture are key.

How to read your inspection report

An inspection report usually groups findings by system and by severity, such as safety issues, major defects, and maintenance items. Focus first on safety and costly items, use photos to understand location, and ask the inspector about anything unclear. No home is perfect, so the goal is an informed decision.
